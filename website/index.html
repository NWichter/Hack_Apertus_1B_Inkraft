Hack Apertus 2026 · Track 1B Swiss Voices

The law is the answer key.

Inkraft asks a language model short questions on Swiss social and health insurance law, in German, French and Italian, as of 1 October 2026. Every gold answer rests on a verbatim quote from the federal statute. Every wrong answer is sorted: was it the rule that applied before an amendment, or was it invented?

How it works View the pitch

One question, two amounts

Swiss social insurance law changes on fixed dates. A model trained on last year's web answers with last year's rule, confidently. For a municipal AHV office, a medical practice or a social service, that is worse than “I don't know”.

  • AHV branch offices in the municipalities, often run by generalists
  • Medical practice staff answering insurance and billing questions
  • Social services and the insured themselves

How Inkraft measures

Gold from the statute

Every answer is anchored to one paragraph of federal law: fedlex ELI with version date and the verbatim quote. The answer stands in that quote or follows from it by a recorded calculation.

Three authentic languages

German, French and Italian versions of federal acts are equally binding. The French and Italian gold answers are taken from those texts, not translated.

An as-of date

For 53 provisions amended since 2024 the item also stores the earlier rule, quoted from the earlier fedlex version. A wrong answer that matches it counts as stale, not as invented.

Credit for “I don't know”

SimpleQA-style grading: correct, incorrect or not attempted. A penalised score rewards abstaining over guessing.

Four outcomes per answer

  • Correct matches the law in force on 1 October 2026
  • Stale matches the rule before an amendment
  • Invented matches neither
  • Not attempted the model declines or refers to an office

Grading you can check

  1. Rules first. Numbers, units and refusals are extracted in all three languages.
  2. Then a panel of 3 open-weight judges from other model families than Apertus (Qwen3-235B-A22B-2507 (Alibaba, open weights), GLM-5.3 (Z.ai, open weights), DeepSeek V3.2 (DeepSeek, open weights)); each graded 61 constructed answers correctly before use, and the majority counts.
  3. Then a human graded a blind 22 % sample of the grades: Cohen's κ [[NIKLAS-PRÜFUNG: kappa_qwen]] against the first judge, [[NIKLAS-PRÜFUNG: kappa_panel]] against the panel majority.
  4. Then make run replays the scoring. It recomputes every number from the stored answers and grades and checks them against the expected results.

Evaluation harness

Package with command line, Docker make run that verifies its own output, and unit tests.

Done

Dataset

327 German core questions, 951 question–answer rows in German, French and Italian, from 19 federal acts and ordinances. 53 questions store the previous rule word for word from the earlier fedlex version. All cores are LLM drafts (98 GPT-5.5 via Codex, 97 Claude, 86 Gemini, 46 the open model Nemotron 3 Ultra). Every row passes the check against fedlex and an independent check by a second model; a human checked a random sample of 35 cores and removed [[NIKLAS-PRÜFUNG: human_drops]].

Done

Exposure check against Apertus training data

The Swiss legislation in MultiLegalPile is part of the Apertus 1.5 training mix. Of the 951 rows, the corpus holds the current wording for 564, only the old wording for 84 and neither for 277. For the 135 rows of temporal questions it holds the current wording 1 times and the old one 76 times.

Done

Results for Apertus 1.5 8B and 70B

Graded by the judge panel: the 70B answers 50.7 % of the rows correctly, the 8B 33.0 %. On the 157 rows whose rule changed and whose old answer is stored, the 70B is right in 7.0 % and gives the old rule in 29.9 %. With the paragraph in the prompt it is right in 98.7 % of the same rows. Adding today's date to the prompt changes nothing.
