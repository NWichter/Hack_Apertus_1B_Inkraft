# Technical report — Inkraft

> Three sentence summary: Swiss residents, AHV branch offices, medical practices and social services need exact amounts, deadlines and responsibilities from Swiss social and health insurance law, and a model that states last year's rule is worse than one that says it does not know. Inkraft is a trilingual (German, French, Italian) short-answer benchmark of <!--n:cores-->327<!--/n--> questions (<!--n:rows-->951<!--/n--> rows) in which every gold answer is backed by a verbatim quote from the authentic text of a federal act as of 1 October 2026, and for <!--n:stale_pairs-->53<!--/n--> amended provisions the previously valid answer is stored verbatim from the earlier Fedlex version, so that wrong answers split into _stale_ (earlier law) and other errors. Graded by a panel of <!--n:panel_size-->3<!--/n--> open-weight judges, Apertus 1.5 70B answers <!--n:correct_70b-->50.7<!--/n--> % of rows correctly but only <!--n:stalepair_correct_70b-->7.0<!--/n--> % of the rows whose rule changed with a stored earlier answer, where it gives the earlier law in <!--n:stalepair_stale_70b-->29.9<!--/n--> %; with the cited paragraph in the prompt it reaches <!--n:openbook_stalepair_70b-->98.7<!--/n--> % on the same rows, and a date in the prompt does not help.

## Use Case Description

Every person living in Switzerland is insured under the Federal Health Insurance Act (KVG) and the old-age and survivors' insurance (AHV). Questions about deductibles, contribution rates, waiting periods, family allowances and registration deadlines reach municipal AHV branch offices, front-office staff in medical practices, social services and the insured themselves. Mistakes cost money: a missed switching deadline binds a person to an insurer for another year, a wrong waiting period delays an invalidity insurance (IV) claim. Public administrations already deploy Apertus [@swissai2026apertus15], so the question is not only whether the model knows this law, but whether it knows the law _in force_.

Social insurance law changes on fixed dates. The minimum child allowance under Art. 5 para. 1 FamZG was CHF 200 per month until 2024 and is CHF 215 since the version of 1 January 2025; the deductible for original drugs with cheaper generics (Art. 38a para. 1 KLV) rose from 20 to 40 %. A model trained on older text may give the earlier figure with confidence. Language models have source-specific effective knowledge cutoffs [@cheng2024dateddata], legal hallucinations are frequent [@dahl2024legalfictions], and outdated statutory knowledge has been shown for German law [@prior2026oldfriend]. Inkraft therefore separates three kinds of failure: an answer that matches the rule before an amendment (_stale_), an answer that matches no version (_other_, including near misses), and an honest refusal (_not attempted_).

## Dataset Summary

Each item is a single-turn question with a short gold answer, accepted variants, _near-miss_ answers (a related but wrong value) and, for amended provisions, the outdated answer. Every item is anchored to one paragraph: Fedlex ELI with version date and the verbatim quote in the language of the item. The as-of date is 1 October 2026 for all items.

## Data Collection Method

**Truth comes from the statute, never from a language model.** The pipeline runs Fedlex → paragraphs → version diff → drafting → automatic checks → independent second check → human spot check → freeze → evaluation. It fetches 20 acts and ordinances from the Fedlex SPARQL endpoint (EOV yielded no item that passed the checks) in German, French and Italian: the consolidation applicable on 1 October 2026, the one applicable at the end of 2023, every version in between and published future versions (`src/fetch_fedlex.py`, URLs and SHA-256 in `data/sources/fedlex_manifest.v2.json`). Articles are split into paragraphs; `src/find_changes.py` diffs every paragraph across versions.

**Drafting.** 303 cores were drafted per paragraph by Claude, GPT-5.5 (Codex) and Gemini in early October; the gold answer must occur verbatim (or as a normalised number) in the paragraph. Because the version diff was the scarce resource, 160 further temporal candidates were drafted by the open model Nemotron 3 Ultra from ranked version diffs (prompt `nemotron-temporal-v2`, hash stored per row), including four found by a numeric diff of acts outside the first manifest (FamZG, EOV, KLV) and checked against the stored Fedlex versions. French and Italian questions are translations, but their gold answers and evidence come from the authentic French and Italian text of the same paragraph, because all three versions are equally binding [@publg2023art14].

**Automatic verification** (`src/validate_items.py`, every row): schema; the evidence occurs verbatim in the cited paragraph of the version valid on the as-of date; the answer occurs in the evidence; the outdated answer occurs verbatim in an earlier stored version and differs from the current value; a near-miss value valid in an earlier version is rejected; calculation items are recomputed from inputs anchored in the cited text; duplicates; language and value consistency across DE/FR/IT; Swiss spelling and a PII scan. **v<!--n:version-->1.0.0-rc1<!--/n-->: <!--n:validator_errors-->0<!--/n--> errors on <!--n:rows-->951<!--/n--> rows**, <!--n:validator_warnings-->216<!--/n--> warnings (most only flag a published later version).

**Release rules** (`src/auto_review.py`, fixed before any model output was scored; decisions logged per core in `data/review/auto/`): existing cores lose paragraph clusters and near-duplicates (19 dropped) and gain proposed answer variants and outdated answers only when the validator passes (21 patched); a candidate needs no validator error, no duplicate and no "reject" in the development pre-assessment, and its correction notes are applied by Nemotron and re-validated (`src/auto_fix.py`). Every remaining core then gets an **independent second check by Qwen3-235B** (`src/second_check.py`): answer supported by the paragraph, unambiguous question, no leak, equivalent languages, valid variants and near misses, and a real (not merely renamed) earlier rule. 15 of 335 cores were flagged; 8 were dropped, 7 calculation items were kept because the checker sees no calculation inputs while the validator recomputes them. Drop rates by drafting model: Codex 4.9 %, Claude 7.6 %, Gemini 9.5 %, Nemotron 71 % (114 of 160 candidates; most changes in the diff pool are renamings such as "ordentliches Rentenalter" → "Referenzalter"). Where the judge panel disagreed on an answer, an open model proposed further answer variants; a variant was added only if its number equals the gold number (or all its words occur in the cited paragraph) and its evidence span is verbatim in the paragraph (`src/panel_fixes.py`, logged with evidence; <!--n:patched_variants_cores-->0<!--/n--> cores). Ambiguity flags were listed, never applied automatically.

**Human check** (`src/draw_human_sample.py`, seed 20261013, `src/import_human.py`). A stratified random sample of <!--n:sample_cores-->35<!--/n--> cores (<!--n:sample_stale-->15<!--/n--> STALE pairs, <!--n:sample_other-->20<!--/n--> others) was checked by the curator against the linked paragraph, blind to model outputs: <!--n:human_drops-->[[NIKLAS-PRÜFUNG: human_drops]]<!--/n--> rejected and removed before the freeze (STALE stratum <!--n:human_err_stale-->[[NIKLAS-PRÜFUNG: human_err_stale]]<!--/n-->, Wilson 95 % <!--n:human_err_stale_ci-->[[NIKLAS-PRÜFUNG: human_err_stale_ci]]<!--/n--> %; other stratum <!--n:human_err_other-->[[NIKLAS-PRÜFUNG: human_err_other]]<!--/n-->, <!--n:human_err_other_ci-->[[NIKLAS-PRÜFUNG: human_err_other_ci]]<!--/n--> %): <!--n:human_dropped_list-->[[NIKLAS-PRÜFUNG: human_dropped_list]]<!--/n-->. All other rows are `auto_verified`; rows a human checked are `reviewed`. No item was selected, changed or removed on model outcomes.

**Exposure check.** Apertus 1.5 was trained on the Swiss legislation subset `switzerland_lexfind` of MultiLegalPile [@swissai2026transparency; @niklaus2024multilegalpile]. For every row we search the verbatim current evidence and, for amended provisions, the earlier evidence in the German, French and Italian files at the published revision of 12 January 2024; we also count 13-gram overlaps between questions and the corpus [@brown2020gpt3; @sainz2023contamination]. The current wording occurs in <!--n:cont_current-->564<!--/n--> rows, only the earlier wording in <!--n:cont_outdated-->84<!--/n-->, neither in <!--n:cont_absent-->277<!--/n--> (<!--n:cont_short-->26<!--/n--> evidences too short). For the <!--n:cont_temporal_rows-->135<!--/n--> `temporal` rows the corpus holds the current wording for <!--n:cont_temporal_current-->1<!--/n--> and the earlier one for <!--n:cont_temporal_outdated-->76<!--/n-->. <!--n:cont_13gram-->55<!--/n--> questions share a 13-gram with the corpus; they reuse statutory phrasing, the questions themselves are new.

**Consent.** No personal data is collected; vignettes use roles only. A consent form template for optional reviewers is provided (`docs/consent_form_template.md`).

## Evaluation

### Method

Grading follows SimpleQA [@wei2024simpleqa]: each answer is CORRECT, INCORRECT or NOT_ATTEMPTED; INCORRECT answers carry the subtype STALE (matches the outdated answer), NEAR_MISS or OTHER. Metrics: correct, incorrect and not-attempted rates, correct given attempted (CGA), F-score, hallucination rate I/(C+I), share of stale errors, penalised scores C − λ·I (λ = 1, 3) [@kalai2025hallucinate; @wen2025abstention], language consistency (same grade in DE, FR and IT) [@qi2023crosslingual] and the open-book gain, which separates a knowledge gap from a reading gap. Intervals are 95 % Wilson or cluster bootstrap over cores (1,000 resamples); paired comparisons use exact McNemar tests.

Grading has three stages. (1) A deterministic pre-grade extracts numbers, number words, units and refusals in all three languages; it is a hint for the judges, never a grade. (2) A **panel of open-weight LLM judges** grades every main-run answer against gold, variants, outdated and near-miss answers with the same prompt (temperature 0) [@zheng2023judging; @panickssery2024selfpreference]: <!--n:panel_judges-->Qwen3-235B-A22B-2507 (Alibaba, open weights), GLM-5.3 (Z.ai, open weights), DeepSeek V3.2 (DeepSeek, open weights)<!--/n-->. None of them drafted a released item or is an examinee. The majority grade counts; without a majority the first judge decides (<!--n:panel_no_majority-->4<!--/n--> of <!--n:panel_rows-->3804<!--/n--> answers). On 61 constructed answers with known grades every judge passes the 0.95 gate (<!--n:panel_calibration-->Qwen3-235B-A22B-2507 61/61, GLM-5.3 61/61, DeepSeek V3.2 61/61<!--/n-->); Mistral Large 3, planned as the European judge, was rate-limited upstream on OpenRouter throughout and was replaced by DeepSeek V3.2. Inter-judge agreement: Fleiss' κ = <!--n:fleiss_kappa-->0.95<!--/n--> (three grades), <!--n:fleiss_kappa_5-->0.89<!--/n--> with subtypes; unanimous on <!--n:panel_unanimous-->97<!--/n--> % of answers; agreement of each judge with the majority: <!--n:panel_agreement-->Qwen3-235B-A22B-2507 99.4 %; GLM-5.3 98.3 %; DeepSeek V3.2 98.7 %<!--/n-->. The German ablation rows are graded by Qwen alone, and the paired date and prompt comparisons use Qwen on both sides. (3) A human graded a stratified random <!--n:judge_share-->22<!--/n--> % sample of the judgements of the submitted run (Apertus 70B, `closed_dated`: <!--n:judge_sample-->211<!--/n--> answers) blind to the grades; French and Italian answers were read with a German machine translation (Apertus 70B) next to the original. Agreement with the first judge (on which the sample was drawn): κ = <!--n:kappa_qwen-->[[NIKLAS-PRÜFUNG: kappa_qwen]]<!--/n--> (95 % CI <!--n:kappa_qwen_ci-->[[NIKLAS-PRÜFUNG: kappa_qwen_ci]]<!--/n-->, raw <!--n:agree_qwen-->[[NIKLAS-PRÜFUNG: agree_qwen]]<!--/n--> %; DE <!--n:kappa_qwen_de-->[[NIKLAS-PRÜFUNG: kappa_qwen_de]]<!--/n-->, FR/IT <!--n:kappa_qwen_frit-->[[NIKLAS-PRÜFUNG: kappa_qwen_frit]]<!--/n-->); with the panel majority κ = <!--n:kappa_panel-->[[NIKLAS-PRÜFUNG: kappa_panel]]<!--/n--> (raw <!--n:agree_panel-->[[NIKLAS-PRÜFUNG: agree_panel]]<!--/n--> %) [@cohen1960kappa; @bavaresco2025llmjudges]. As a diagnostic Apertus 70B also graded the `closed_dated` runs: it agrees with Qwen (κ <!--n:selfpref_kappa_70b-->0.86<!--/n--> on 70B answers, <!--n:selfpref_kappa_8b-->0.93<!--/n--> on 8B answers) but marks <!--n:selfpref_extra_70b-->48<!--/n--> more of its own answers correct, against <!--n:selfpref_extra_8b-->19<!--/n--> on the 8B's answers, consistent with self-preference; it is never used for scores.

## Dataset Limitations

Only federal law; cantonal rules are excluded. Items are verified by deterministic checks against Fedlex, an independent model check and a human spot check of <!--n:sample_cores-->35<!--/n--> cores, not by a full human review; the second check flagged little (15 of 335), so it is a weak filter for subtle ambiguity. <!--n:closed_drafted-->281<!--/n--> of <!--n:cores-->327<!--/n--> cores were drafted by closed models (Claude, GPT-5.5, Gemini), disclosed per row; accuracy by drafting model is reported in `data/results.json`, but drafting model and question type are confounded (all calculation and false-premise items come from Gemini, most STALE pairs from Nemotron). French and Italian questions are model translations; answers come from the authentic texts; the French and Italian part of the human judge check relied on a machine translation. One annotator, not a native Swiss speaker. <!--n:yes_no_share-->20<!--/n--> % yes/no items (50 % guessing rate, reported separately). The STALE-pair set is limited by the version diff: most changes since 2024 are renamings or cross-reference renumbering, not new values. The as-of date ages; <!--n:future_change-->29<!--/n--> cores already have a published later version. The exposure check covers only the Swiss legislation subset of MultiLegalPile, not web crawls [@su2025nemotroncc; @penedo2025fineweb2]. Short answers measure knowledge, not the quality of advice.

## Lessons Learnt and Recommendations

For the model: a date in the system prompt does not make Apertus more current, while the cited paragraph fixes almost every error, so the fix has to come from data or retrieval: a refreshed legislation corpus, retrieval with a version filter instead of parametric recall [@prior2026oldfriend], and training abstention instead of rewarding guesses [@kalai2025hallucinate]. For the dataset: yearly updates on 1 January from the version diff, native-speaker review of French and Italian, a cantonal extension. For benchmark builders: the scarce resource is not drafting but real value changes; a paragraph diff over all intermediate versions plus a verbatim check of the earlier answer found <!--n:stale_pairs-->53<!--/n--> usable STALE pairs, a panel of judges from different model families makes grading less dependent on one model, and the examinee as judge was measurably lenient with itself.

`make run` (repository root) builds a Docker image (Python 3.12, no GPU, no API keys, a few minutes) and replays the scoring offline: it recomputes every result block from the stored responses (`data/responses/`) and the panel's grades (`data/judgements/`) and compares them with `data/expected_results.json` (PASS/FAIL). `make test` runs the unit tests in the same image. `make run MODE=full` evaluates any OpenAI-compatible model given `LLM_BASE_URL`, `LLM_API_KEY`, `LLM_NAME` and grades with `JUDGE_*` (smoke test: `LIMIT=50`). `scripts/run_after_freeze.sh` reruns calibration, generation and judging; `src/panel.py` combines the judges; `report/analysis.py`, `report/make_figures.py` and `report/fill_numbers.py` recompute every number and the figure in this report. The dataset construction is scripted end to end (`src/fetch_fedlex.py` → `find_changes.py` → `gen_nemotron.py` → `validate_items.py` → `auto_fix.py` → `auto_review.py` → `second_check.py` → `draw_human_sample.py` / `import_human.py` → `after_review.py` → `panel_fixes.py` → `freeze.py`). Seeds: 20261001 (generation), 20261016 (bootstrap), 20261013 (human samples).
